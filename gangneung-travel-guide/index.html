Gangneung Travel Guide: Ojukheon, Beaches, Coffee and Chodang Tofu

By Real Korea Trip Editors✓ Last checked: Oct 30, 20268 min read

Quick answer

  • Gangneung is an east-coast city of beaches, hanok estates and coffee. Start with Ojukheon, birthplace of the mother and son on Korea's 50,000- and 5,000-won notes (adults ₩3,000; last entry 17:00).
  • Eat Chodang sundubu, soft tofu traditionally set with seawater, and try a coffee at Anmok Beach, where Gangneung's coffee culture started with vending machines.
  • Get there by KTX-Eum: adult standard-class fares are ₩27,600 from Seoul Station or ₩26,000 from Cheongnyangni (Korail fare table as of Sep 1, 2026). These include a temporary discount that ends when faster running starts on the Cheongnyangni–Seowonju section.

Gangneung, on Korea's east coast at the end of the KTX-Eum Gangneung Line, pairs beaches with Joseon-era hanok estates and a food scene UNESCO named a Creative City of Gastronomy in 2023. Start with Ojukheon, birthplace of Shin Saimdang and her son Yulgok, the mother and son on Korea's 50,000- and 5,000-won notes (adults ₩3,000; last entry 17:00), then Seongyojang (adults ₩5,000), Gyeongpo Beach and a bowl of Chodang sundubu.

Source: Ojukheon Municipal Museum (Korean), Korea Heritage Service, Seongyojang (Korean), Gangneung City (Korean) and Korea Tourism Organization; checked Oct 30, 2026.

Ojukheon

Ojukheon is the house where Shin Saimdang (1504–1551) and her son Yi I, the Confucian scholar known as Yulgok (1536–1584), were born, the Korea Heritage Service says; Yulgok was born in its Mongryongsil room. It's a Treasure (designated 1963), named for the black bamboo (ojuk) around the house. The Korea Tourism Organization calls it the birthplace of the figures on Korea's banknotes, and the city museum's currency gallery displays the portraits of Yulgok and Saimdang by the artist who drew the 50,000- and 5,000-won note designs.

HoursEntry 09:00–17:00, then you can stay until 18:00
ClosedOpen year-round; on Jan 1, Seollal and Chuseok Day the house is open but the indoor exhibition halls close

Source: Ojukheon Municipal Museum — visitor information, Ojukheon and currency gallery (Korean); Korea Heritage Service; Korea Tourism Organization; checked Oct 30, 2026.

Local tip: Arrive by 17:00. Ojukheon's museum says no one is admitted after the entry time, even though the grounds stay open until 18:00.

Seongyojang

Seongyojang is a Joseon-era noble family house that grew over time rather than being built to a single plan; its Yeolhwadang hall dates from 1815. The name means "boat-bridge manor," after the village people once reached by boat. Descendants have lived here for 10 generations over more than 300 years, the heritage service's signboard text says.

Hours09:00–18:00 in summer · 09:00–17:00 in winter. The site's general footer gives a 17:30 ticket cutoff, which doesn't fit winter closing, so confirm the winter last-entry time before a late visit
AdmissionAdults ₩5,000 · discounts (teenagers, 65 and over and others) ₩3,000 · children ₩2,000
Guided commentaryOn the hour 09:00–16:00 (not 12:00–13:30), 30–40 minutes; the site doesn't say which languages
RulesNo pets except guide dogs, no outside food, no drones without permission

Source: Seongyojang and fees and guiding (Korean) and Korea Heritage Service (Korean); checked Oct 30, 2026.

Local tip: Seongyojang is still a family home, and it posts one-day temporary closures on its notice board (it closed on Oct 20, 2026, for example). Check its notices (Korean) before you go.

Gyeongpodae, Gyeongpo Beach and Anmok coffee street

  • Gyeongpodae Pavilion: first built in 1326 and later moved and rebuilt, it's a pavilion that poets, calligraphers and painters loved to visit, the Korea Heritage Service says. It became a Treasure in 2019, and the Korea Tourism Organization singles it out for cherry blossom season.
  • Gyeongpo Beach: about 1.8 km of sand (Gangneung City), which KTO calls one of the most famous beaches on Korea's east coast; Gangneung City lists it as free to visit.
  • Anmok Beach coffee street: Gangneung's coffee culture began with instant coffee from the vending machines at Anmok, the city says; today there are hundreds of specialty cafés around Gangneung, and the city's coffee festival has run since 2009.

Source: Korea Heritage Service — Gyeongpodae, Korea Tourism Organization — course and coffee festival (Korean), Gangneung City — Gyeongpo Beach and coffee (Korean); checked Oct 30, 2026.

Coming for the first sunrise of the year? Gangneung City notes that many visitors come to Gyeongpo Beach for the New Year sunrise and recommends booking a room early. Look for the city's New Year event notice in December.

Local tip: "Made with seawater" has a modern twist. A Korea Tourism Organization food guide says Chodang tofu today is made with deep-sea water drawn from more than 200 m down, so ask the restaurant if you're curious.

Gangneung Danoje Festival

UNESCO describes Gangneung's Danoje as a four-week-long festival around Dano, with a shamanistic ritual on the Daegwallyeong ridge, folk songs and a mask drama. UNESCO proclaimed it a Masterpiece of the Intangible Heritage in 2005 and inscribed it on the Representative List in 2008. Its dates follow the lunar calendar: in 2026 the main festival ran June 15–22. The 2027 dates haven't been announced yet; watch the festival committee's site (Korean).

Source: UNESCO and Gangneung Danoje Festival Committee (Korean); checked Oct 30, 2026.

Getting there and around

By trainTake a Gangneung-bound KTX-Eum from Seoul Station or Cheongnyangni. Trains bound for Jeongdongjin and Donghae follow a different branch and don't stop at Gangneung Station, so check the destination when you book (tickets open at 07:00, two months ahead)
KTX fares (adult)From Seoul Station: standard ₩27,600 · premium class (udeung: fare plus seat surcharge) ₩33,100. From Cheongnyangni: standard ₩26,000 · premium class ₩31,200
Fare conditionUntil faster running starts on the Cheongnyangni–Seowonju section, Korail charges this section at a temporary discount. After that, its table shows standard-class fares of ₩30,900 from Seoul and ₩29,200 from Cheongnyangni. Check the fare shown when you book
In townUse Naver Map for buses or taxis between Ojukheon, Gyeongpo and Anmok
PayingBring a card and some won: see paying in Korea

Before you go

  1. Book your KTX-Eum to Gangneung (adult standard class ₩27,600 from Seoul Station, ₩26,000 from Cheongnyangni) and check the fare shown for your train.
  2. Reach Ojukheon by 17:00 and check Seongyojang's notices for closures.
  3. Visiting on the last Wednesday of the month? Ojukheon is half price.
  4. Coming for New Year's sunrise? Book a room early.
  5. Install Naver Map for buses and taxis.

Source: Ojukheon Municipal Museum (Korean), Seongyojang (Korean) and Gangneung City; checked Oct 30, 2026.

FAQ

What is Gangneung known for?

Its beaches, the hanok estates of Ojukheon and Seongyojang, Chodang soft tofu and coffee. UNESCO lists its Danoje Festival and named it a Creative City of Gastronomy in 2023.

How do I get from Seoul to Gangneung?

Take the KTX-Eum on the Gangneung Line from Seoul Station or Cheongnyangni. Korail's fare table (as of Sep 1, 2026) lists adult standard-class fares of ₩27,600 from Seoul and ₩26,000 from Cheongnyangni, including a temporary discount that ends when faster running starts on the Cheongnyangni–Seowonju section. Check times and the fare shown when you book.

How much is Ojukheon?

Adults pay ₩3,000, teenagers ₩2,000 and children ₩1,000; people aged 65 and over get in free with ID. Last entry is 17:00.

What is Chodang sundubu?

Soft tofu from Gangneung's Chodang village, traditionally set with seawater; a Korea Tourism Organization guide describes the village's tofu as now made with deep-sea water, so ask your restaurant.

When is the Gangneung Danoje Festival?

It follows the lunar calendar, so the dates change each year. In 2026 it ran June 15–22; the 2027 dates haven't been announced yet.
