Jjimjilbang for First-Timers: Korean Spa Rules, Prices and Etiquette

By Real Korea Trip Editors✓ Last checked: Oct 31, 20266 min read

Quick answer

  • A jjimjilbang is a Korean bathhouse with heated sauna rooms and shared rest areas. Baths are separated by gender; in the shared sauna rooms everyone wears the provided clothes.
  • Your locker key works like a wallet: tap it for snacks and extras, then pay at the counter when you leave. Entry prices at three operators we checked ranged from ₩22,000 to ₩26,000 for adults.
  • Check hours and time limits first. Some places close at 22:00, and at the three we checked, entry covers a base of 4 to 8 hours before hourly charges, so a jjimjilbang isn't automatically a cheap overnight stay.

A jjimjilbang is a Korean bathhouse with gender-separated baths plus heated sauna rooms and rest areas you share in provided clothes; at three operators we checked on Oct 31, 2026, adult entry cost ₩22,000–₩26,000, with base time limits of 4 to 8 hours before extra charges. You keep your things in a locker, use the key to pay for snacks and extras, and settle up at the counter when you leave. Two rules from Korean law are worth knowing: children aged 4 and over can't go into the opposite-sex bath, and everyone wears the provided clothes in shared sauna rooms.

Source: Public Health Control Act, Enforcement Rule Annex 4 (Korean), Korea Tourism Organization, Aquafield, Centum Spaland and Spa Lei; checked Oct 31, 2026.

How a visit works

  1. Pay at the counter and get a locker key. The Korea Tourism Organization says it's usually worn on your wrist or ankle: keep it with you.
  2. Change in the locker room for your gender. Korea.net's bathhouse guide describes taking a light shower and soaking in the warm bath before a scrub.
  3. Put on the provided clothes for the shared areas. In a sauna room men and women share, Korean law requires the operator to have you wear them.
  4. Try the sauna rooms, starting with cooler ones, as KTO suggests. Keep each session short and follow the notices posted by each room.
  5. Eat and rest in the shared hall: roasted eggs and sikhye are the classics.
  6. Return the key at the counter and pay for any extras you charged to it.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization (column last updated November 2024), Korea.net and Enforcement Rule Annex 4 (Korean); checked Oct 31, 2026.

Local tip: Your locker key is also your tab. KTO says it lets you use the snack bar, massage chairs and other paid extras, and you pay for all of it in one go when you hand the key back.

Body scrub (seshin)

Many bathhouses offer an exfoliating scrub. In Korea.net's bathhouse episode, bathers tell the scrub attendant how many people and which body parts, then soak in the warm bath first so dead skin comes off more easily; the attendant calls them by locker number. Some bathhouses may ask for payment upfront, so ask at the counter about the price.

Source: Korea.net — Seshin and bathhouse culture (Nov 2019); checked Oct 31, 2026.

Before you go

  1. Check the place's hours, time limit and extra-hour charge.
  2. Leave large luggage at your hotel, or check whether the place has suitcase lockers.
  3. Bring a little cash and a card: see paying in Korea.
  4. Travelling with a child aged 4 or over? Plan for a same-gender adult to take them into the bath.
  5. Keep your locker key on your wrist the whole time.

Source: Operators' sites above and Korea Tourism Organization; checked Oct 31, 2026.

FAQ

What is a jjimjilbang?

A Korean bathhouse with gender-separated baths plus heated sauna rooms and rest areas that men and women share in provided clothes.

How much does a jjimjilbang cost?

At three operators we checked on Oct 31, 2026, adult entry was ₩22,000–₩26,000, with extra hourly charges after a base of 4 to 8 hours.

Can you sleep overnight at a jjimjilbang?

At a place that's open 24 hours, yes, but time limits may add hourly charges. Korean law doesn't allow bedding for overnight stays, just large towels and pillows for a short rest.

Do you have to be naked in a jjimjilbang?

In the gender-separated baths, Korea.net's guide describes bathers removing their clothes. In the shared sauna rooms and rest areas, everyone wears the clothes provided.

Can children go into a jjimjilbang?

It depends on the place: Centum Spaland, for example, admits children from elementary-school age, and they must be with a guardian of the same sex. By law, children aged 4 and over can't enter the opposite-sex bath.
