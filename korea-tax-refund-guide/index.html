Tax Refund in Korea: How Visitors Get Money Back on Shopping

By Real Korea Trip Editors✓ Last checked: Oct 23, 20268 min read

Quick answer

  • Foreign visitors staying in Korea for less than 6 months can claim back VAT on goods bought at stores that show a Tax Refund sign, for purchases of ₩15,000 or more in a single transaction.
  • Instant refunds at the till work at participating instant-refund stores: show your passport, and each purchase must be at least ₩15,000 and under ₩1,000,000, up to ₩5,000,000 in instant-refund purchases in total after you arrive.
  • For general refunds, keep the goods available for inspection: at Incheon Airport, start at a refund kiosk and show the unopened goods to customs if it asks. If the goods will go in a checked bag, tell the airline at check-in and complete the kiosk/customs export-clearance steps before handing that bag over; collect the airport refund afterwards as instructed.

Foreign visitors staying in Korea for less than 6 months can get part of the VAT back on purchases of ₩15,000 or more in a single transaction at stores showing a Tax Refund sign: roughly 5–8% of the price, after fees. At participating instant-refund stores, show your passport to check eligibility: each purchase must be at least ₩15,000 and under ₩1,000,000, with no more than ₩5,000,000 in instant-refund purchases in total after arrival. For general refunds, keep the goods available for inspection. At Incheon Airport, start at a refund kiosk and show the goods to customs if instructed. If they will travel in checked baggage, tell the airline at check-in and complete the kiosk/customs export-clearance steps before handing that bag over.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — tax refund guide and the Presidential Decree in force from Jan 2, 2026 (Korean); checked Oct 23, 2026.

Who can claim

Foreign visitors staying in Korea for less than 6 months generally qualify, as do overseas Koreans who have lived abroad for at least 2 years and are staying in Korea for 3 months or less. You can't claim if you're staying 6 months or longer, work in Korea, or are a diplomat or US military personnel stationed in Korea. Purchases made under a company name or through someone else don't count either.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — comprehensive tax refund guide; checked Oct 23, 2026.

What you can claim on

Most goods qualify: clothes, shoes, bags, cosmetics, perfume, electronics, ginseng. A few things don't. Cooked food and services such as massages are out, because customs can't inspect them when you leave. So are items you've opened or used before taking them out of Korea, anything bought duty-free, and goods that are banned anyway.

Two practical rules matter more than the list. First, only stores that offer tax refunds count, and they show a "Tax Refund" sign with the refund company's logo. Second, an ordinary receipt isn't enough: ask for the tax refund documents when you pay, and keep them until you leave.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — comprehensive tax refund guide; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Local tip: Keep tax-free purchases sealed until you're home. Items opened or used before you take them out of Korea aren't eligible, so leave the packaging sealed.

Instant refund: the easy way

At stores that offer it, you show your passport and pay the price with the tax already taken off. In the official guide, the steps for an instant refund end there: check eligibility with your passport, pay the lower price, then leave Korea as normal. The limits are set in law: each purchase must be under ₩1,000,000, and your instant-refund purchases since arriving can't exceed ₩5,000,000 in total. The store can deduct an approved processing fee from the tax amount.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — comprehensive tax refund guide and the Presidential Decree (Korean); checked Oct 23, 2026.

General refund: pay now, claim later

Everything else works the other way round. You pay the full price, take the refund voucher, and claim the money later in one of four ways:

  • Downtown: at a staffed refund center or an unmanned kiosk in the city, for single purchases of ₩6,000,000 or less. Bring your passport, the voucher and a credit card. You still have to get the customs check when you leave.
  • At the airport: through the refund kiosk and, if needed, the customs check, then at a refund counter or kiosk.
  • By mailbox: at ports without a refund counter, drop the customs-confirmed voucher in the mailbox, with your details and refund method written on it.
  • By mobile: submit the customs-confirmed voucher through the refund company's app or website.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — comprehensive tax refund guide; checked Oct 23, 2026.

At the airport: the order that matters

For general refunds, the goods need export clearance before they leave with you, and customs may ask to inspect them. Incheon Airport describes the order like this:

  1. Check in. The airport advises carrying refund-eligible items and their documents with you rather than checking them.
  2. If they are in a checked bag, tell the airline staff at check-in. The bag is tagged for customs inspection and you take it back.
  3. Scan your passport at the refund kiosk in the departure hall. If the kiosk says your items need a customs inspection, show your passport, the unopened and unused goods and the purchase confirmation at the customs desk.
  4. A checked bag that customs has cleared goes onto the oversized baggage belt next to the customs desk.
  5. After security and immigration, collect the refund at the refund counter or kiosk in the duty-free area, or use the mailbox or mobile option.

Customs confirmation is described as an essential requirement: without it, there's no refund. If you miss it, the Korea Tourism Organization says you can still claim on a later trip, if you bring the goods and the voucher back within the voucher's validity period and get the customs check when you leave Korea again.

Incheon Airport lists these refund counters, both in the 3rd-floor duty-free area (after security and immigration):

How much you actually get back

Don't expect the full 10% VAT. The Korea Tourism Organization puts the refund at roughly 5–8% of the purchase price after the refund company's processing fee, depending on the price, the company and how you're paid. Taking the refund on a credit card or by mobile payment adds a separate fee, so taking cash at the counter avoids that extra fee. On a ₩100,000 purchase, 5–8% works out to about ₩5,000–₩8,000.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — comprehensive tax refund guide (FAQ); checked Oct 23, 2026.

Our take: Where a store offers it, the instant refund is usually simpler, because the tax comes off when you pay. Check the net price and any processing fee, keep your purchase records, take the goods out within the required period, and follow any customs instructions. Use the general refund when an instant refund isn't available or a purchase is over the limits.

Duty-free or tax refund?

They're different systems. Duty-free shops at airports and in the city sell goods without customs duty or consumption taxes. Tax refund stores sell at normal prices and give back the VAT and individual consumption tax afterwards. For general refunds there's no overall spending limit, just the ₩15,000 minimum per purchase; instant refunds have the limits above. Either way, when you take purchases to another country, that country's import and duty-free rules apply. Korea's US$800 traveler allowance is for goods brought into Korea, not a universal allowance for taking purchases home.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — comprehensive tax refund guide (FAQ) and Korea Customs Service — customs clearance guide for passengers; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Before you shop, and before you fly

  1. Carry your passport when you shop.
  2. Look for the "Tax Refund" sign, and spend ₩15,000 or more in one transaction.
  3. Ask for the tax refund documents, not just a receipt.
  4. Keep tax-free goods unopened and unused.
  5. At the airport, finish the refund kiosk and any customs inspection before your goods leave your hands.
  6. Take the goods out of Korea within 3 months of buying them.

FAQ

What is the minimum purchase for a tax refund in Korea?

₩15,000 in a single transaction at a tax refund store. The amount is set in Korea's enforcement rules, in force from Jan 2, 2026.

What are the limits for an instant tax refund?

Each purchase must be under ₩1,000,000, and your instant-refund purchases since arriving in Korea can total up to ₩5,000,000.

Do I need to show my purchases at the airport?

For general refunds, keep the goods with you until export clearance is done. At Incheon you scan your passport at the refund kiosk; if it asks for a customs inspection, show your passport, the unopened goods and the purchase confirmation at the customs desk. For instant refunds, follow any customs instructions you are given.

Can I get a tax refund with my receipt?

No. You need the tax refund documents that the store issues with your purchase.

How much VAT do I get back in Korea?

About 5–8% of the purchase price after processing fees, according to the Korea Tourism Organization. A refund to a card or by mobile payment carries an extra fee.
