K-ETA Explained: Do You Need One to Visit Korea?

By Real Korea Trip Editors✓ Last checked: Oct 23, 20267 min read

Quick answer

  • K-ETA is an online travel authorization for people who can enter Korea without a visa. Where it's required, you need approval before you board your flight or ship.
  • 22 countries and regions are temporarily exempt until December 31, 2026 (Korea time), including the US, Canada, the UK, Japan and Australia. Check the official list, then confirm on the K-ETA site.
  • Apply only on the official site (k-eta.go.kr) or the K-ETA app. The fee is ₩10,000 (about US$7–8, plus a separate online payment fee), and approval usually takes up to 72 hours.
Recent changes to know
  • Dec 23, 2025Korea's Ministry of Justice extended the temporary K-ETA exemption for currently exempt countries and regions until December 31, 2026 (KST).

If you can visit Korea without a visa, you've probably seen the letters K-ETA and wondered whether they apply to you. Short answer: maybe not right now. Korea runs the K-ETA system, but some countries and regions are temporarily exempt until the end of 2026 — and the official site is the place to check which side of that line you're on.

What K-ETA is (and isn't)

K-ETA is Korea's Electronic Travel Authorization. It's for travelers from countries and regions whose citizens can enter Korea without a visa. Where it applies, you need approval before you board your flight or ship — the official guide warns that applying after boarding, or once you're already in Korea, can lead to refusal or entry restrictions.

It isn't a visa. The site is clear that an approved K-ETA doesn't guarantee entry: the final decision is made by an immigration officer when you arrive. And it doesn't change how long you can stay. The K-ETA validity is separate from your visa-free stay — for example, the official list shows 90 days for US citizens and 6 months for Canadians.

Source: K-ETA — application guide and eligible countries; checked Oct 23, 2026.

The temporary exemption, until December 31, 2026

Korea's Ministry of Justice has extended a temporary K-ETA exemption for one more year: it runs from January 1 to December 31, 2026 (Korea time) and applies to the countries and regions that were already exempt.

The extension notice itself doesn't list the countries, but the Korea Tourism Organization publishes the list of 22 countries and regions covered through December 31, 2026:

  • Asia: Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Macau
  • North America: United States (including Guam), Canada
  • Europe: United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Belgium, Denmark, Austria
  • Oceania: Australia, New Zealand

Check that official list, and confirm your own status on the K-ETA site before you travel: if you're exempt, you'll see a pop-up message when you scan the photo page of your passport during the application.

Even if you're exempt, you can still choose to apply — the notice mentions benefits such as not having to fill in an arrival card. In that case you pay the fee, and it isn't refunded. The temporary K-ETA exemption alone does not exempt you from the arrival card. If you travel without a valid K-ETA, check whether you need to submit an e-Arrival Card; other exemptions, such as holding a valid Korean Residence Card, may apply. The official service is free.

Source: K-ETA — notice on extension of temporary exemption (posted Dec 23, 2025), Korea Tourism Organization — visa & travel requirements (updated Sep 18, 2026) and e-Arrival Card — who needs to submit; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Our take: If you'll be entering Korea in 2027, check the latest official notice before you travel. The extension notice cited here confirms the temporary exemption only through December 31, 2026 (KST).

Who doesn't need to apply

The official FAQ lists people who are exempt from applying, including:

  • Holders of a valid Korean passport (including dual nationals)
  • Registered foreigners and people with a Korean visa
  • Children aged 17 and under, and adults aged 65 and over, on the date of entry
  • Transfer passengers — unless you need to enter Korea because of an itinerary change or to collect your baggage
  • Cruise travelers
  • Flight attendants and seafarers, ABTC (APEC) card holders, UN passport holders, diplomatic and official passport holders, and active-duty US service members under the US–Korea SOFA

Source: K-ETA — FAQ, "Who is eligible for the application exemption?"; checked Oct 23, 2026.

How to apply

Have these ready: a valid passport, an email address you can check, an ID photo, and a credit or debit card for the fee. Then:

  1. Go to k-eta.go.kr (or the official K-ETA app) and choose to apply as a member or a non-member.
  2. Enter your passport details — this is the point where exempt travelers see the pop-up.
  3. Enter your travel details, check everything, and pay the ₩10,000 fee.
  4. Wait for the result. The site says it typically takes 72 hours or less, but it can take longer when applications spike, and there's no expedited service.

Source: K-ETA — official website, application guide and what to prepare; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Local tip: Apply well before your flight rather than counting on the 72-hour figure, and make sure the details you enter match your passport exactly. The site warns that false or inaccurate information can get an approval refused or cancelled.

Watch out for fake K-ETA sites

The official site carries a warning about look-alike agencies and phishing sites that charge high fees for "agency" or "expedited" K-ETA applications. The site says applications are accepted only at www.k-eta.go.kr or through the K-ETA mobile app, and it offers no paid fast track.

Source: K-ETA — official website and application guide; checked Oct 23, 2026.

After you're approved

  • Use it again. A K-ETA can be used for multiple trips during its 3-year validity (or until your passport expires, if sooner).
  • Skip the arrival card — when you enter on your K-ETA. If you also hold a Korean visa and enter on that visa, the official e-Arrival Card guide says you must complete an e-Arrival Card.
  • New passport, new K-ETA. If your passport is reissued — or your criminal record or infectious disease information changes — you need to apply again.
  • Keep your travel details up to date. If your purpose of visit, address in Korea, contact details, travel dates or companions change, update them on the official site.

Source: K-ETA — application guide and e-Arrival Card — who needs to submit; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Before you fly

  1. Check the official exempt list, then confirm on k-eta.go.kr — scan your passport and see if the exemption pop-up appears.
  2. Using the temporary exemption without a valid K-ETA? Check whether you need to submit the free e-Arrival Card, unless another arrival-card exemption applies.
  3. If you need a K-ETA, apply on the official site or app and pay ₩10,000.
  4. Allow time for processing before your flight.
  5. Plan your arrival: Incheon Airport to Seoul, getting online and paying in Korea.

Source: K-ETA — official website and exemption notice; checked Oct 23, 2026.

FAQ

Do I need a K-ETA to visit Korea in 2026?

It depends on your passport. K-ETA is for people who can enter Korea without a visa, but 22 countries and regions — including the US, Canada, the UK, Japan and Australia — are temporarily exempt until December 31, 2026 (KST). The K-ETA site shows a pop-up if you're exempt when you scan your passport.

How much does a K-ETA cost?

₩10,000 (about US$7–8), plus a separate online payment fee. It isn't refunded if your application is refused.

How long does K-ETA approval take?

The official site says typically 72 hours or less, though it can take longer. There's no expedited service.

How long is a K-ETA valid?

3 years, or until your passport expires if that's sooner. You can use it for multiple trips, but each stay is still limited to your visa-free period.

Do children need a K-ETA?

Children aged 17 and under on the date of entry are on the official list of people exempt from applying, as are adults aged 65 and over.
