Korean Etiquette for Travelers: Restaurants, Table Manners and the Subway

By Real Korea Trip Editors✓ Last checked: Oct 25, 2026Updated: Oct 31, 20265 min read

Quick answer

  • Tipping is generally not expected. The Korea Tourism Organization says tipping isn't required or expected in Korea, and asking for more water or the complimentary side dishes costs nothing extra.
  • At the table, follow the oldest person. Korea.net's guide says younger people usually wait for elders to start eating, and use both hands when pouring or receiving a drink from someone older.
  • On the subway, let people off first and keep your voice down. Seoul Metro also asks riders to offer seats to pregnant women, people with children and the physically disadvantaged.

Three basics for visitors to Korea: tipping is generally not expected (the Korea Tourism Organization says it isn't required or expected), follow the oldest person's lead at the table, and on the subway let people off first and keep your voice down. Korea.net's guide also says many households eat quite casually. If you're unsure, follow your host's lead. Below: restaurants, table manners and Seoul Metro's 10 subway rules, each from an official source.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization — About Korean Food, Korea.net — table manners and Seoul Metro — etiquette; checked Oct 25, 2026.

Table manners

Korea.net, the government's official English site, runs a guide to table manners for visitors. Its main points:

SituationThe custom
Setting the tableRice on the left and soup on the right; the spoon on the left and chopsticks on the right
Eating with eldersYounger people usually wait until the older people at the table pick up their spoons and start
Drinks with eldersHold the cup or bottle with both hands when pouring for or receiving from someone older, and turn slightly away when you drink
ChopsticksDon't stick them upright in a bowl of rice: that is done in rituals for the dead
BowlsDon't lift your rice or soup bowl while eating
MannersDon't pick your teeth or blow your nose during a meal

The same guide notes that not talking during meals was the etiquette in the past, and that many households now eat quite casually.

Source: Korea.net — While in Korea, Episode 7: Table manners (culture series, Sep 12, 2019); checked Oct 25, 2026.

Our take: If you're unsure, watch the oldest person at the table and follow their pace. Using both hands for a drink with an older person is an easy gesture to get right.

On the subway

Seoul Metro lists 10 basic rules for riders:

  1. Don't push and shove on a crowded train.
  2. Let passengers get off the train before you board.
  3. Offer your seat to pregnant women, people with children and the physically disadvantaged.
  4. Don't walk or run on an escalator.
  5. Put your phone on vibrate and keep your voice down on calls.
  6. Don't buy things from peddlers on trains.
  7. Don't run or speak loudly on trains.
  8. Fold your newspaper for others, or take it with you when you get off.
  9. Keep to the right when walking in stations.
  10. Don't try to ride without paying.
Local tip: Seoul Metro also asks riders not to rush for a train when its doors are about to close. Wait for the next one instead, and before you step on, check that the train is heading toward your destination.

Source: Seoul Metro — Subway etiquette; checked Oct 25, 2026. Fares and transfers: Seoul subway guide.

Before you go

  1. Tipping is generally not expected, so there's no need to budget for it.
  2. At a meal with older people, let them start first, and use both hands for drinks.
  3. Don't leave chopsticks standing in your rice.
  4. On the subway, let people off first, keep your voice down and keep to the right in stations.
  5. Need help explaining something? The 1330 Korea Travel Helpline interprets daily from 07:00 to midnight Korea time.

Source: Korea Tourism Organization, Korea.net, Seoul Metro and our emergency help guide; checked Oct 25, 2026. Bathhouse manners: our jjimjilbang guide.

FAQ

Do you tip in Korea?

No. The Korea Tourism Organization says tipping is not required or expected, and extra water or side dishes don't cost more.

What should I not do with chopsticks in Korea?

Don't stick them upright in a bowl of rice. Korea.net's guide explains that this is done in rituals for the dead.

How do I drink with older people in Korea?

Hold the cup or bottle with both hands when pouring for or receiving from someone older, and turn slightly away when you drink.

What are the rules on the Seoul subway?

Seoul Metro asks riders to let passengers off first, offer seats to pregnant women, people with children and the physically disadvantaged, keep their voice down and keep to the right in stations.
