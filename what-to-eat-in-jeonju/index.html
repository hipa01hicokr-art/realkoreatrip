What to Eat in Jeonju: Bibimbap, Bean Sprout Soup and Nambu Market

By Real Korea Trip Editors✓ Last checked: Oct 23, 20265 min read

Quick answer

  • Start with Jeonju bibimbap. Jeonju City describes it as more than 30 ingredients in one bowl, with rice cooked in beef bone broth and Jeonju's own bean sprouts.
  • Then try kongnamul gukbap (bean sprout soup with rice). The city recommends it with a poached egg, sesame oil and roasted laver, and pairs it with moju, a herbal liquor.
  • Go to Nambu Market for pisundae, the early-morning Dawn Goblin Market (go before 9 a.m.) and the market's youth mall and night market.

Jeonju is a UNESCO City of Gastronomy. If you only have a day, three things are worth knowing about: Jeonju bibimbap, the city's bean sprout soup with rice, and Nambu Market. Here's what each one is, how it's served, and where to find it.

Jeonju bibimbap

Jeonju City calls bibimbap a representative K-food dish and Jeonju its home. Jeonju City describes its version as more than 30 ingredients in one bowl, arranged in five colors, and lists what makes it different:

  • rice cooked in rich beef bone broth
  • Jeonju's crunchy bean sprouts
  • yellow muk jelly, which the city says is made with water from the Nokdupo spring at Omokdae
  • gochujang and soy sauce fermented the traditional way

The city says bibimbap restaurants cluster around the old town, the Hanok Village, Jeonju City Hall and Jeonju's three major markets. One it highlights is Gajok Hoegwan, opened in 1979 by Kim Neon-im, Jeonju's first designated food master, and now run by the third generation. The bowl comes with side dishes, bean sprout soup and steamed egg.

Source: Visit Jeonju — Jeonju bibimbap; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Local tip: Restaurants change. Before you go, search the restaurant's Korean name in Naver Map to check that it's open that day and how to get there.

Kongnamul gukbap: bean sprout soup with rice

This is Jeonju's other signature bowl. In the Nambu Market style, the city explains, boiled bean sprouts and rice are cooled in the bowl, then a hot broth made from dried anchovies and kelp is poured in and out over them. It's eaten with a poached egg, sesame oil and roasted laver (gim). Locally it's known as a haejang meal, the kind of soup people eat the morning after drinking.

The traditional partner is moju, a traditional alcoholic drink boiled with herbs, which Jeonju City's food guide pairs with kongnamul gukbap. Treat it as a cultural food pairing, not a hangover treatment, and skip it if you're driving or avoiding alcohol.

Source: Visit Jeonju — Nambu Market; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Nambu Market

Nambu Market is the main traditional market of the Jeonbuk region, beside Pungnammun, the old city gate that was rebuilt after a fire in 1767. It sits near the Hanok Village, and since the 2000s its youth mall and night market have drawn visitors from the village.

Three things to look for there:

  • Pisundae, Jeonju-style sausage: pig intestine stuffed with vegetables, meat and blood, but no glass noodles. The city says it's softer and thicker than sundae elsewhere, and it comes with chives and vinegared red pepper paste.
  • The Dawn Goblin Market (Dokkaebi Market), an early-morning street market by Jeonjucheon Stream next to Nambu Market, with fresh produce and snacks like fried doughnuts and noodles. Jeonju City's dedicated market page lists 4–9 a.m., while its Nambu Market page says 4–10 a.m., so aim to arrive before 9.
  • Kongnamul gukbap, the market's best-known meal (see above).

Source: Visit Jeonju — Nambu Market and Daybreak Market; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Local tip: Several of these dishes contain meat, broth or egg (beef bone broth in bibimbap rice, anchovy broth in the soup, pig intestine in pisundae). If you avoid certain foods, ask before you order.
Our take: If your day trip starts early, have kongnamul gukbap near Nambu Market for breakfast and save bibimbap for lunch in the Hanok Village. It's two of Jeonju's best-known dishes without rushing.

Why Jeonju is known for food

Jeonju is a member of UNESCO's Creative Cities Network as a City of Gastronomy. UNESCO's page calls it the "Taste City" and points to rice from the Honam Plain and seafood from the Yellow Sea as the base of its food culture.

Source: UNESCO Creative Cities Network — Jeonju; checked Oct 23, 2026.

FAQ

What is Jeonju famous for eating?

Jeonju bibimbap (the city calls itself "the home of bibimbap") and kongnamul gukbap (bean sprout soup with rice). Jeonju is a UNESCO City of Gastronomy.

What makes Jeonju bibimbap different?

Jeonju City lists rice cooked in beef bone broth, Jeonju bean sprouts, yellow muk jelly, and traditionally fermented gochujang and soy sauce, with more than 30 ingredients in one bowl.

What is moju?

A traditional alcoholic drink boiled with herbs, which Jeonju City's food guide pairs with kongnamul gukbap. It's a cultural pairing, not a hangover treatment, and it contains alcohol.

When is the Dawn Goblin Market open?

Early in the morning. Jeonju City's dedicated market page lists 4–9 a.m. and its Nambu Market page says 4–10 a.m., so arrive before 9 to be safe.
