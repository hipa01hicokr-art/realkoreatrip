How to Book KTX Train Tickets in Korea as a Foreigner

By Real Korea Trip Editors✓ Last checked: Oct 23, 20268 min read

Quick answer

  • Book in English on Korail's official site for foreigners (korail.com/global/eng) or in the KORAIL+ app. The normal booking window opens at 07:00 (Korea time) two months before your travel date; Seollal and Chuseok holiday trains follow a separate schedule.
  • KTX and SRT merged on September 1, 2026. The government says they now run as KTX, with fares on average 10% lower than before. Seoul–Busan (via Gyeongju) is ₩54,400 in standard class.
  • Keep your email and password — that's how you look up a ticket booked on the foreigner site, and you'll need them to cancel online before departure.
Recent changes to know
  • Sep 1, 2026KTX and SRT began running as one KTX service. The government says KTX fares were adjusted to former SRT levels (about 10% lower on average), and integrated trains are booked through KORAIL+ and the Korail website.

What changed in September 2026

Korea used to have two high-speed operators: Korail ran the KTX, and a separate company, SR, ran the SRT from Suseo Station. After the companies were merged, the government says the two started running as KTX from September 1, 2026. Three things matter for visitors:

  • One place to book. Integrated trains are booked through the KORAIL+ app and the Korail website. KORAIL+ replaced Korail's old app, Korail Talk.
  • Lower fares. The government says KTX fares were brought down to former SRT levels — about 10% lower on average.
  • More seats on trains from Suseo. Most of the added trains run on the Suseo routes, which the government says have about 30% more seats.

Source: Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport, Aug 31, 2026 and Aug 3, 2026 (Korean); checked Oct 23, 2026.

Booking online, in English

  1. Go to korail.com/global/eng — Korail's official site for foreigners. (The old letskorail.com address now redirects to korail.com.)
  2. Search your departure station, arrival station, date and number of passengers, and pick a train.
  3. Enter an email address and a password. Korail says you need to type them exactly — including upper- and lower-case letters — to look up your ticket later, and suggests writing them down.
  4. Pay by credit card.
  5. To check or cancel, open MY TICKET and enter the same email and password.

Source: Korail — official site for foreigners and ticketing guide; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Local tip: Save the email and password in your phone's notes the moment you book. Without them you can't pull up the ticket on the foreigner site.

When tickets go on sale

Korail says the normal booking window opens at 07:00 (Korea time), two months before the departure date. Online, you can buy until the train leaves; ticket machines stop selling 10 minutes before departure, and station counters sell until departure.

The big exception is Korea's two main holidays. Seollal and Chuseok holiday trains can have their own reservation schedule and rules — for Chuseok 2026, Korail ran a separate booking period that required membership, then opened remaining seats later, when non-members could buy and pay straight away. Check Korail's latest holiday notice if you're travelling around those dates.

Our take: For Friday-to-Sunday trips, book as soon as your normal booking window opens. Those are also the days with higher cancellation fees, so pick your train with care.

Source: Korail — ticketing guide and 2026 Chuseok reservations notice; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Buying at the station

You can also buy at ticket machines or the counter in a train station. Note that this means a railway station — Korail says KTX tickets aren't sold at subway stations.

Source: Korail — ticketing guide; checked Oct 23, 2026.

If your plans change

Before departure, you can cancel online — in MY TICKET for tickets booked on Korail's English website. After the train has left, the English guide directs you to a station counter. Korail's Korean guide also lists an exception: KTX tickets bought through KORAIL+ can be refunded in the app up to 10 minutes after departure, if it's confirmed that you're not on the train. Follow the refund options for the channel you bought from. Refunds aren't possible after the arrival time printed on the ticket.

The fee depends on when you cancel and on the day of travel. "The day before" means the previous calendar date, not a rolling 24-hour period:

Is the KORAIL PASS worth it?

The KORAIL PASS is a rail pass for foreign visitors. The current select passes let you choose 2, 3, 4 or 5 travel days within a 10-day period from the pass's starting date:

PassAdult (13+)Child (6–12)
2-day select₩131,000₩66,000
3-day select₩186,000₩93,000
4-day select₩234,000₩117,000
5-day select₩275,000₩138,000

You need a valid passport and a credit card to buy it, and the details must match your passport. Choose your travel dates in MY TICKET before reserving seats; seat reservations open two months before departure, up to two seat tickets per person per day. Board with your pass, your passport and, if you reserved one, your seat reservation. Refunds are possible only before the pass's starting date.

Korail's current pass guide lists metropolitan railways and temporary tourist trains as not included. An older notice from 2024 also excluded SRT trains, which have run as KTX since September 2026. If your plans use trains from Suseo Station, check that a seat reservation is available for that train when you book, or ask Korail's call center (+82-1599-7777).

Source: Korail — About KORAIL PASS, pass reservation and older pass notice; checked Oct 23, 2026.

Our take: Do the sums for your own trip. A 2-day pass costs ₩131,000, while a Seoul–Busan return in standard class is ₩108,800 (₩54,400 each way via Gyeongju) — so for one return trip, regular tickets are cheaper. A pass can make sense when you're fitting several long trips into a short window.

Before you travel

  1. Decide your stations — trains to Busan leave from Seoul Station and Suseo Station, among others.
  2. Book from 07:00 (Korea time) two months ahead for weekend trips — and check Korail's holiday notice if you're travelling around Seollal or Chuseok.
  3. Save your booking email and password.
  4. Check you'll have data on the day for the app or your ticket — see eSIM vs SIM card vs pocket Wi-Fi.

FAQ

Can foreigners book KTX tickets online?

Yes. Korail runs an English site for foreigners at korail.com/global/eng, and tickets can also be bought in the KORAIL+ app, at ticket machines and at station counters.

How far in advance can I book the KTX?

Korail's normal booking window opens at 07:00 (Korea time), two months before the departure date. Seollal and Chuseok holiday trains can follow a separate schedule.

How much is the KTX from Seoul to Busan?

₩54,400 for an adult in standard class from Seoul Station via Gyeongju, and ₩52,900 from Suseo Station, according to Korail's fare table effective September 1, 2026. Other routes between the two cities can cost a different amount.

Is the SRT still separate from the KTX?

No. The government says the KTX and SRT started running as one KTX service on September 1, 2026, with integrated trains booked through KORAIL+ and the Korail website.

Can I get a refund on a KTX ticket?

Yes. Before departure you can cancel online; after departure, generally at a station counter (KORAIL+ KTX tickets can be refunded in the app up to 10 minutes after departure if you're not on the train). No refunds after arrival. Fees depend on timing and the day of travel. For non-holiday Monday–Thursday departures, cancellation more than three hours before departure is free. Public holidays and the designated Seollal/Chuseok travel periods use the holiday fee schedule.
